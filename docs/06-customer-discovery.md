# 06 — Customer Discovery

Judges ask "how do you know people want this?" The answer is **interviews, surveys, and a
pilot**. Goal by the January submission: **20+ interviews, 50+ survey responses, 5–10 pilot
users.** Keep a spreadsheet: name/initials, age range, date, key quotes, pain score (1–5),
would-pay price.

## Rules for good interviews

- Ask about **their past behavior**, not about your idea ("Tell me about your last doctor's
  visit" beats "Would you use a device that…").
- Don't pitch until the last 5 minutes.
- Write down **exact quotes** — they go straight into your narrative and pitch.
- Always ask permission, and tell them how you'll use their answers.
- Talk to seniors in person when possible (senior centers, grandparents' friends, church
  groups, libraries). Bring cookies.

## Script A — Seniors (20 minutes)

1. Tell me a little about yourself. How do you spend a typical week?
2. How many doctors or specialists do you see? When was your last appointment?
3. Walk me through that appointment. What did the doctor tell you?
4. After you got home, how much did you remember? Did you ever need to call back?
5. Has a medication ever been changed and you weren't sure how to take it? What happened?
6. Do you take notes, bring someone along, or ask for printouts? How well does that work?
7. Does anyone in your family help you keep track of appointments or medicines? How?
8. Have you ever received a call you think was a scam? What happened?
9. What technology do you use every day? What's frustrating about it?
10. *(Now show the prototype or a photo.)* If this recorded your appointment and gave you a
    simple summary — what do you like? What worries you?
11. Would you feel comfortable asking your doctor, "Is it OK if I record this?"
12. What would you expect something like this to cost? Would you buy it, or would you want
    your family to?

## Script B — Adult children / caregivers (15 minutes)

1. Who do you help care for? How far away do they live?
2. How do you find out what happened at their doctor's appointments?
3. Tell me about a time something got missed — a medication, an appointment, a bill.
4. How much time per week do you spend on caregiving tasks? What's the hardest part?
5. Have they ever been targeted by a scam? What happened?
6. What products or services have you tried (medical alert, pill organizers, apps)?
7. *(Show the concept.)* Would a summary of their important conversations help? What would
   you want to see?
8. Would your parent accept this? What would make them say no?
9. Would you pay $79 for the device? $9.99/month for the family dashboard? What would make
   it worth it?

## Script C — Experts (doctors, nurses, pharmacists, senior center staff)

1. How much of what you tell older patients do you think they remember?
2. What happens when they forget? (missed doses, readmissions, repeat calls)
3. How would you feel if a patient recorded the visit? What would make it OK?
4. Would you recommend something like this to patients or families?
5. Would you write us a short letter of support? *(Ask this at the end, if it went well.)*

## Survey (Google Forms, share with caregivers online + in person)

1. Your age range / the age of the person you care for
2. How many doctor visits per month? (0 / 1 / 2–3 / 4+)
3. "After appointments, I (or my parent) forget important details." (Strongly disagree → Strongly agree)
4. "I often don't know what happened at my parent's appointments." (1–5)
5. Has a missed medication change or appointment caused a problem? (Y/N + describe)
6. Has your family been targeted by a phone scam in the last year? (Y/N)
7. How interested would you be in SEEN? (1–5) [include a 2-sentence description + photo]
8. What's the most you'd pay for the device? ($29 / $49 / $79 / $129 / $199)
9. Would you pay a monthly fee for the family dashboard? (No / up to $5 / up to $10 / up to $20)
10. Email if you'd like to join the waitlist (optional) → this becomes your traction number!

## Pilot plan (December)

- **Who:** 5–10 older adults (ideally with a family member who agrees to view the dashboard).
- **Consent:** signed consent form from each participant (see `08-privacy-legal-ethics.md`),
  reviewed by your advisor. Participants can delete any recording, any time.
- **Setup:** give each a charged SEEN, show them the button (2 minutes), put the dashboard
  on the family member's phone.
- **Duration:** 1–2 weeks.
- **Measure:**
  - Recordings per user per week
  - Summary accuracy rating (1–5), asked for each summary
  - "How easy was SEEN to use?" (1–5)
  - "Would you be disappointed if you couldn't use SEEN anymore?" (Very / Somewhat / Not) —
    40%+ "very" is a strong product-market-fit signal
  - Would you buy it? At what price?
  - Best quote + a photo (with permission) for the pitch
- **Fix and repeat:** change one thing based on feedback, and tell that story in the pitch
  ("Margaret couldn't feel the buzz, so we doubled the vibration strength").
