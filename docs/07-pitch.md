## 2. Written concept narrative (3–5 pages)

Suggested outline and page budget:

1. **The Problem** (¾ page) — story of one real person from your interviews (use a first name
   or pseudonym), then 3–4 cited stats. End with the problem statement from `02-product-spec.md`.
2. **Our Customers** (½ page) — the senior (user) and adult child (buyer); interview + survey
   numbers ("In 23 interviews, 19 said…").
3. **Our Solution: SEEN v1** (1 page) — how it works in 4 steps, a photo of the prototype, a
   screenshot of the dashboard. Design principles. What's working today.
4. **Why SEEN is Different** (½ page) — the competitor table from `05-business-model.md`.
5. **Business Model & Economic Viability** (¾ page) — pricing, unit economics, go-to-market,
   market size.
6. **Traction & Progress** (½ page) — prototype built, pilot results, AI accuracy test, letters
   of support, waitlist.
7. **Privacy & Responsibility** (¼ page) — press-to-record, consent card, family sharing is
   opt-in, not an emergency or medical device.
8. **Our Team** (½ page) — each member's role and relevant skills; advisor and mentors.
9. **Next Steps** (¼ page) — milestones and how you'd use prize money.

Writing tips: short paragraphs, bold key numbers, cite sources as footnotes, one image per
page max, have your advisor and one English teacher proofread.

## 4. The 5-minute pitch script (timing guide)

| Time | Speaker | Section |

| 0:00–0:40 | CEO | **Hook + problem.** Start with a story: "Last spring, my grandmother came home from a cardiologist visit with a new prescription — and no idea how to take it…" |
| 0:40–1:10 | CEO | **Customer + evidence.** Interviews, stats, the user/buyer insight. |

| 2:10–2:40 | Software lead | **What makes it different.** Senior-first design principles, scam warnings, privacy by design. |
| 2:40–3:30 | Design/Ops | **Business model + market.** |
| 3:30–4:20 | CEO | **Traction.** Pilot results, a user quote, AI accuracy test, partners. |

Rehearse with a timer until you hit 4:45 consistently (buffer for nerves and demo hiccups).
Everyone should be able to do the demo, in case someone is sick.

## 5. Judge Q&A prep (3 minutes)

Practice answering each in ≤ 30 seconds.

| Likely question | Answer outline |
|---|---|
| Isn't recording people illegal? | Press-to-record only, never always-on. In all-party-consent states, the user must ask — SEEN comes with a consent card ("I use SEEN to help me remember. Is it OK if I record?"), and the start buzz/light makes recording obvious. |
| What about HIPAA? | HIPAA governs doctors and insurers, not patients recording their own visits. When we partner with healthcare organizations, we'll become a HIPAA business associate and meet those requirements. Data is private by default; family sharing is opt-in. |
| What if the AI gets a medication wrong? | Instructed to copy doses exactly and never invent; full transcript is always one tap away; tested at X% accuracy; family double-check; we say "always confirm with your pharmacist." Not a medical device. |
| Why not just use Pocket or a phone app? | Our interviews: X% of seniors don't install apps. Pocket is built for professionals; we built for Margaret. |
| Will seniors actually use it? | Pilot data: X recordings/week, ease-of-use score Y/5. One button, no screen. |
| Who pays? | Usually the adult child. Later, senior living facilities and Medicare Advantage plans. |
| How do you make money on AI costs? | ~$3–5/user/month in AI costs vs $9.99 plan; Batch API and our own transcription cut that further. |
| What stops a big company from copying you? | Trust + distribution with senior centers and care providers, senior-specific design data from our pilots, and speed. Also — big companies build for everyone; we build for one customer. |
| What's the hardest part ahead? | Certification (FCC) and manufacturing — that's why we'd use prize money for a PCB run and pre-compliance testing. |
| Will you actually keep working on this? | Our next 3 milestones and dates. |
