SALTBACK SIMULATION - STEP 1: ONE STORM, IN PLAIN ENGLISH
============================================================

THE STORM: road surface at 22F (-6C), with a 0.2 mm ice film
stuck to the road after plowing. 20% of the salt bounces off the road.

1) HOW MUCH SALT THIS STORM NEEDED
   About 307 lb per lane-mile. Any less leaves some ice; any more melts nothing extra.

   Salt spread      Ice cleared   Salt wasted   Money wasted (per lane-mile)
     100 lb/lane-mi      33%           0%        $ 0.00
     200 lb/lane-mi      65%           0%        $ 0.00
     300 lb/lane-mi      98%           0%        $ 0.00
     400 lb/lane-mi     100%          23%        $ 4.17
     500 lb/lane-mi     100%          39%        $ 8.67
     600 lb/lane-mi     100%          49%        $13.17
     700 lb/lane-mi     100%          56%        $17.67
     800 lb/lane-mi     100%          62%        $22.17

   At double the needed amount (~620 lb), 50% of the salt does nothing but pollute.

2) TEMPERATURE CHANGES EVERYTHING
   At 30F: 1 lb of salt melts 52.5 lb of ice -> this storm needs   62 lb/lane-mile
   At 25F: 1 lb of salt melts 14.9 lb of ice -> this storm needs  217 lb/lane-mile
   At 20F: 1 lb of salt melts  8.9 lb of ice -> this storm needs  366 lb/lane-mile
   At 15F: 1 lb of salt melts  6.4 lb of ice -> this storm needs  504 lb/lane-mile
   At 10F: 1 lb of salt melts  5.1 lb of ice -> this storm needs  632 lb/lane-mile
   Same storm, very different salt needs. A fixed 'one rate for everything' habit wastes salt
   on mild days and under-treats cold ones. That is why Saltback starts from pavement temperature.

3) CAN THE CHEAP SENSOR SEE IT?
   Storm-average runoff: the probe stayed in range 40 of 40 times, and
   Saltback's chloride estimate was off by 5% on average. It found 95% of the salt
   that actually reached the road.  -> The DFR0300 works IF it measures well-mixed storm water
   (for example, a collection bucket). Step 2 shows what happens when it logs live in the drain.
   Without the temperature probe, readings would be 52% too low (cold water conducts
   less electricity). -> The $5 DS18B20 is essential, not optional.

4) WARNING FOR THE HARDWARE PLAN
   The first rush of brine off the road maxed out the probe in 40 of 40 runs (it is
   far saltier than the 20 mS/cm limit). So the DFR0300 cannot measure the first flush. Options:
   measure the storm-average water (works, see #3), or add a higher-range (K=10) probe later.

5) IS THE MODEL TRUSTWORTHY?
   It was checked against the widely published 'ice melted per pound of salt' chart: within
   5% from 0F to 25F. (Near 30F it reads higher, because tiny temperature changes
   near freezing matter a lot.) The model never reads that chart; it uses freezing-point physics.



STEP 2: THE SAME KIND OF STORM, HOUR BY HOUR
============================================================

THE STORM: 4 in of snow over 8 h with the road at 22F.
The first truck arrives after 0.5 h, then returns every 2 h:
4 passes x 150 lb = 600 lb per lane-mile.
Afterwards the road warms to 38F and the leftover salt washes away.

SALTBACK STORM CARD
   Route (10 lane-mi) | Salt applied: 6,000 lb | Estimated excess: 47% | Chloride runoff: HIGH | Next comparable storm: -40%

6) WAS THE ROAD SAFE?
   Icy (bonded ice on a freezing road) for 0.2 h. With no salt: 11.4 h.
   Just 80 lb per pass would have kept it icy only 0.5 h. The other 47% of the salt bought only 15 extra minutes of bare road.

7) WHERE THE SALT WENT
   67% washed into the storm drain, 33% was thrown onto the shoulder and soil
   (bounce, tires, wind). None of it disappears: it all ends up in streams or groundwater.

8) WHAT LEFT THROUGH THE DRAIN
   The drain ran for 32 h. Peak chloride 39,074 mg/L, storm average 7,981 mg/L.
   For scale: the EPA's safe limit for fish and insects in streams is 860 mg/L short-term and
   230 mg/L long-term. A stream dilutes drain water, but chloride never breaks down, so it builds up.

9) DID THE SENSOR KEEP UP?
   The probe was maxed out for 9.9 h, so Saltback only saw 48% of the salt.
   The brine coming off a freezing road is far too salty for the DFR0300 (20 mS/cm max).
   A high-range K=10 probe fixes this: set probe_max_mS_cm to 200 and run again.

NOT IN THE MODEL YET (coming in later steps):
   - a whole winter of storms, and Saltback learning from each one
   - uncertainty: what if our guesses (ice, runoff, bonding speed) are wrong?
   - real lab data from Experiments 1 and 2
