    How it works

    Saltback combines a drain sensor, the town's truck logs and a physics model. Every number on this site comes from the model below, run on simulated storms with typical published values. None of it is real town data.

    From storm to recommendation

    1. MeasureA low-cost conductivity and temperature sensor in a storm drain measures how salty the runoff is after each storm.
    2. CombineSaltback adds the truck log (how much salt was spread, and when) and the weather (road temperature, snowfall).
    3. ModelA physics model steps the storm every 5 minutes: snow falls, salt dissolves, brine melts ice or refreezes, traffic packs snow into ice, runoff reaches the drain.
    4. Find the right amountSaltback tries every salt rate and finds the smallest one that keeps the road within 15 minutes of the best possible result.
    5. Check safetyThe recommended rate must be no more than 6 minutes icier than what the crew actually did, both in the best estimate and in a pessimistic case.
    6. LearnOver a season, the sensor shows how much salt really reaches the drain. Saltback adjusts its model of the route and the crew rate card.

    Import storm records (CSV)

    One row per storm per route. Imported storms replace the example season; your simulator runs are kept.

    Required columns: date, pavement_temp_F, snowfall_in, storm_hours, salt_per_pass_lb. Optional: route, lane_miles, first_pass_h, plow_every_h, temp_after_F, probe_max_mS_cm, measured_drain_salt_lb (salt the sensor accounted for, whole route), probe_maxed_h.
