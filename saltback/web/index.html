How it works

  1. The box hangs on the inside wall of the catch basin, just below the grate and out of the water.
  2. The probes sit in the sump inside a drilled PVC tube that keeps out leaves and grit.
  3. Every 5 minutes the ESP32 wakes, reads conductivity and water temperature, and corrects for temperature.
  4. Readings are saved to the memory card and sent over the cell network after each storm.
  5. Saltback turns conductivity into chloride, matches it with the truck log and weather, and builds the storm report.

Building it

  • Print the box, lid and probe holder in PETG or ASA (PLA softens in summer heat). 3 walls, 25% infill, no supports.
  • Measure your probes before printing the holder; hole sizes are at the top of device/build_parts.py.
  • Coat the soldered board with conformal coating; drain air is wet and salty.
  • Ask the town's public works department before putting anything in a storm drain.

Decision rules

          
    
            
  • Needed salt: the smallest rate within 15 minutes of the best icy time any rate reaches.
    • 
            
  • Saltback's rate: needed + 10%, raised until the road is no more than 6 minutes icier than the logged result, also in a pessimistic case (faster runoff, more brine needed, faster bonding).
    • 
            
  • Next storm: cuts of at most 25%; increases not capped.
    • 
            
  • Still icy over 2 hours at 600 lb per lane-mile: salt alone can't fix it. Below 15°F: sand or a stronger chemical.
