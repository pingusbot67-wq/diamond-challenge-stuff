    How the model works, and what it assumes

    Road. Every 5 minutes, snow falls, trucks plow and spread salt, and salt crystals dissolve into the snow (slower when it is colder). The brine then melts ice, or refreezes into black ice, until it is exactly as salty as the road temperature requires. That rule comes from the NaCl freezing-point table in the CRC Handbook.

    Ice. Traffic packs snow into ice bonded to the pavement unless a brine film protects it. A plow lifts loose snow but not bonded ice. The road counts as icy when bonded ice is thicker than 0.05 mm.

    Drain and sensor. Brine runs off into the drain, mixed with water from unsalted ground. Chloride is converted to the conductivity the probe would read at that water temperature, with ±3% noise, a ±0.5°C temperature-probe error, and the probe's maximum.

    Needed salt. The smallest salt amount per pass that keeps the road within 30 minutes of the safest result any amount can achieve. Saltback recommends that amount plus 10%.

    All inputs are typical published values, not data from a real town. The least certain ones are how fast traffic packs snow into ice and how fast brine runs off. Experiments 1 and 2 can measure both.

    Saltback · Diamond Challenge 2027 · Social Innovation. The model code is in the saltback/ folder of the project repository, in Python and JavaScript versions that give identical results.
