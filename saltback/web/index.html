    How the model works

    Every five minutes snow falls, trucks plow and spread salt, and crystals dissolve into the snow, more slowly when it is colder. The brine melts ice, or refreezes into black ice, until it is exactly as salty as the road temperature requires (NaCl freezing-point table, CRC Handbook).

    Traffic packs snow into ice bonded to the pavement unless at least 0.04 mm of brine protects it. A plow lifts loose snow but not bonded ice. The road counts as icy above 0.05 mm of bonded ice.

    Brine runs into the drain, mixed with water from unsalted ground. The probe reading includes ±3% noise, a ±0.5°C temperature error and the probe's maximum.

    Needed salt is the smallest amount per pass that keeps the road within 30 minutes of the safest result any amount can achieve. Saltback recommends that plus 10%.

    All inputs are typical published values, not one town's data.

    Saltback System

    Takes each storm's truck log and drain-probe readings, works out what the storm actually needed, and turns the gap into feedback a public works crew can act on.
